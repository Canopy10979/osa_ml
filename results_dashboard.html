Why this is trustworthy Scrambling the answers collapsed performance to chance (0.489) — the model wasn’t memorizing leaked information. Re-running with different random seeds moved the score by less than 0.005. The gap between 0.838 and 0.489 is large and real.

The headline correlation

r_hf breathing-linked heart rate swing
−0.46
−1.0 (predicts apnea)0+1.0 (predicts normal)
What r_hf means in plain terms Your heart normally speeds up and slows down in rhythm with your breathing. In healthy sleep that fluctuation is strong. During apnea minutes it collapses. That single measurement alone reaches 0.793 — nearly the whole model — and its negative sign points exactly the direction physiology says it should. This is a genuine biological signal, not a statistical artifact.

The honest catch — where the skill actually comes from

Real event detection spotting individual apneas
~60%
Subject recognition “this person is a severe apneic”
~40%
Inflated headline About 40% of the model’s apparent skill is just recognizing who the person is and riding their overall severity — not spotting individual events. True per-event detection skill is meaningfully smaller than 0.838 suggests.

It can grade severity — it cannot rule OSA out

23 with OSA
4 without
Has OSA — 23 of 27 No OSA — 4 of 27 (only 2 true healthy controls)
The blind spot The model correctly identified only 1 of the 4 non-OSA people. With just 2 genuine healthy controls in the whole cohort, it never learned what “healthy” looks like. Useful for grading severity in people who already have OSA; near-useless for telling a healthy person they are fine.

4 · cross_dataset

⚠️ Mislabeled — this is not OSA

The most impressive-looking numbers in the whole project: 97% accuracy, Random Forest with recall 1.0, and a file literally named model_accuracy_by_osa_status.csv. None of it is about OSA.

What it is actually predicting The target column is Sleep_Label, and the generating code says so outright — regenerate_pipeline.py:121 labels the axis “Sleep_Label (0 = awake, 1 = asleep).” The source file is a sleep-stage scoring sheet (W, N1, N2, N3, R). It is an asleep-vs-awake classifier that was relabeled as an OSA detector.

Problem 1 — the 97% is an illusion

Reported accuracy
0.97
Guess “asleep” every time majority class
0.94
Balanced accuracy the fair measure
0.698
Barely better than doing nothing 94% of the windows are “asleep,” so a model that always answers “asleep” already scores 94%. The honest number, reported in the same folder, is 0.698.

Problem 2 — the correlations prove it isn’t OSA

Heart rate
Drops naturally when you fall asleep — 72 bpm awake → 61 bpm asleep
Correlation−0.41
Importance0.445
SpO₂ (blood oxygen)
The defining sign of apnea — and the model ignores it entirely
Correlation0.066

The smoking gun A real OSA model would lean heavily on oxygen desaturation. This one gives SpO₂ an importance of 0.0019 — essentially zero — while heart rate does nearly all the work. That is exactly the signature of a sleep detector, not an apnea detector.

Problem 3 — one person, one night

1 subject · 1 recording · 925 windows
Zero ability to generalize Everything the model learned came from a single recording of a single individual. It cannot be expected to transfer to anyone else.
Bottom line: dataset_apnea_ecg is the only dataset that supports an OSA claim — and even there the headline is inflated by subject identity and it cannot rule OSA out. The other three are undefined, empty, or measuring the wrong thing.
